## ⚠️ Critical scope rule – read first
PA-25-253 lists **"developing innovative diagnostic technologies and devices" as NON-RESPONSIVE**: applications built around developing new diagnostic technology are returned without review. **Do not present this as "we will build an AI model".** Present it as **cancer-control / health-care delivery research**: *using* AI-assisted, smartphone-based screening to improve how early oral cancer is found and referred. In-scope examples NCI gives include using **information technology to improve evidence-based screening**, mobile apps, telemedicine, and AI in cancer epidemiology.

Good framing examples:
- "Feasibility, acceptability and accuracy of AI-assisted oral cancer screening delivered by primary-care staff in a 4-hospital Thai network"
- "Reducing time-to-diagnosis for oral cancer through mobile-photo triage and tele-referral"

Use an **existing or lightly adapted** AI model. Fund model building through NRCT, PMU or TSRI, not here.

## ⚠️ Partner-hospital structure (NIH policy since 2025)
NIH no longer allows **foreign subawards** in new applications. If a Thai hospital is the applicant, **paying the other 3 Thai hospitals through subawards is likely not allowed**. Options:
1. **Lead hospital holds all funds.** Partner hospitals are **unfunded collaborators** (letters of collaboration). Study staff at partner sites are paid as **consultants** or through the lead's own payroll if feasible.
2. A **US university applies** as prime using NIH's **PF5 international-collaboration structure** (each Thai hospital is an International Project Component). Check whether PA-25-253 accepts this, or a companion PF5 opportunity is required.
3. **Email the NCI programme officer** listed in the NOFO and ask which structure they accept. **Do this before writing.**

# PART 1 – Fill-in template for PA-25-253

## Specific Aims (1 page)
> **Opening paragraph:** Oral cancer burden in Thailand (CP §4); ____% diagnosed late; the gap is a shortage of trained screeners and slow referral in ____ provinces.
> **Long-term goal:** a scalable, AI-assisted early detection pathway integrated into Thai primary care.
> **Objective of this R21:** to test the feasibility, acceptability and real-world accuracy of AI-assisted photo screening delivered by ____ at 4 hospitals and their catchment primary-care units.
> **Aim 1 (feasibility & acceptability):** ____, measured by recruitment rate, completion, usability score (SUS), and screener/patient acceptability.
> **Aim 2 (accuracy in practice):** sensitivity/specificity of AI-assisted screening vs specialist exam ± biopsy in ____ participants.
> **Aim 3 (care pathway):** time from screen to specialist visit and biopsy; referral completion; cost per screen.
> **Expected outcome:** effect sizes and procedures for a larger R01 trial (→ template 18).

| Section | What to write | From |
|---|---|---|
| Significance | Burden; late diagnosis; why existing screening fails in Thailand; relevance to cancer control and disparities (rural, low-income, high-risk) | CP §4 |

| Approach | Design, setting (4 hospitals), participants, sample size/power, screening procedure, reference standard, outcomes, analysis, timeline, pitfalls & alternatives, rigor (blinding of reference-standard assessors, pre-specified analysis) | CP §6, §8 |

**Is it a clinical trial (NIH definition)?** Answer the 4 questions: (1) human participants? (2) prospectively assigned to an intervention? (3) designed to evaluate the intervention's effect on participants? (4) health-related biomedical or behavioural outcome? **All yes = clinical trial.** A screening-delivery study that measures referral completion or time to diagnosis is often **yes**. A pure diagnostic-accuracy study often **no**. Choose a NOFO that allows your answer.

## C. Foreign Justification – fill-in
> "This project must be conducted in Thailand because (1) oral cancer incidence and late-stage presentation are high, with distinctive risk factors (betel quid, tobacco, alcohol) that are rare in US populations; (2) our 4-hospital network provides access to ____ patients per year and histopathology confirmation; (3) the delivery model (non-specialist, AI-assisted, tele-referral) addresses resource constraints common to LMICs, and findings will inform screening for underserved US populations and globally; (4) the team has unique expertise ____."

6. **Oversight:** PI + data manager ____

## E. Review criteria (NIH Simplified Framework)
- **Factor 1 – Importance of the research** (significance, innovation): scored 1–9
- **Factor 2 – Rigor & feasibility** (approach): scored 1–9
- **Factor 3 – Expertise & resources** (team, environment): sufficient / not sufficient
- Plus human-subjects protections, inclusion, budget

## F. NIH rules to remember
- Max **6 applications per PI per calendar year**. Applications **substantially developed by AI** are not treated as original (NOT-OD-25-132)
- Foreign indirect costs **≤ 8%**; foreign applicants use the **detailed** budget
- **No foreign subawards** in new applications (see the warning above); ask the programme officer
- Contact the **programme officer** named in the NOFO before writing, by email with a 1-page summary
