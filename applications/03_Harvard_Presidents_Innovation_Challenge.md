### Venture name & one-liner
**Kon Pop Detection.** AI-assisted smartphone screening that helps community health volunteers catch oral cancer early.

### The problem
Oral cancer kills about 177,000 people a year, mostly in South and Southeast Asia, where betel quid, tobacco and alcohol are common. Caught early it is usually curable; caught late, survival is poor and treatment costs about five times more. In Thailand, the first and often only screening is done by village health volunteers with about one day of training and a paper form. Dentists are scarce, referrals are slow and unsorted, cancers are missed, and patients make long, unnecessary trips.

### The solution
A volunteer app plus a dentist dashboard. Volunteers record risk factors and take three mouth photos. Our AI (fine-tuned Google MedGemma + YOLO lesion detector) flags suspicious areas and scores risk. Dentists see a ranked list and bring high-risk patients in first. It runs on cheap phones, copes with patchy internet, and comes with a picture-based training manual and a training game.

### Customer & market
- **Users:** village health volunteers and hospital dentists. **Buyers:** hospitals, Health Region offices, the Thai Ministry of Public Health and National Health Security Office, then health ministries and insurers across South and Southeast Asia.
- **Market sizing (from our deck):** TAM US$2.4B (oral cancer diagnosis), SAM US$1.1B (South Asia), SOM US$75M. **[FILL: show how each number was calculated (number of people screened × price, or diagnostic spend) and cite the sources. Judges will ask.]**
- **Beachhead:** Thailand. More than one million village health volunteers already deliver community screening, so we sell into a programme that already exists.

### Competition & advantage
- **Status quo:** paper forms and an unaided eye
- **Other mobile/AI oral screening:** e.g. MeMoSA (Malaysia), academic models
- **Our edge:** built for community conditions (cheap phones, variable light) and trained on images from those settings; fits the existing volunteer workflow; triage dashboard for scarce dentists; local clinical leadership and hospital partners; foundation-model approach.
